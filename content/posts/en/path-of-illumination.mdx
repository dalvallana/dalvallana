This time it was Rome's turn. I got to travel to the ancient city in mid-July, and honestly, it's wonderful. Unfortunately, I couldn't enjoy it in all its splendor because of the goddamn heat that batters _bella Roma_ in summer. Still, the fucking blazing sun didn't stop me from walking the mysterious and challenging **Path of Illumination**. You know I'm a geek and I love stories. Well, I loved Dan Brown's [_Angels & Demons_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angels_%26_Demons), and from the day I read it I told myself: I have to go to Rome. A good number of years later (six, I think), I made it.

The first altar of science was the church of Santa Maria del Popolo, where, in the novel, the first _preferiti_ is killed by being choked with earth.

I got to stop by the Vatican. Whether you're a believer or not, it's well worth a visit. St. Peter's Square is one of the largest squares in the world and, by the way, the second altar of science. Air is the element behind this horrific death: the second _preferiti_ has his lungs punctured.

Of course, you can't miss the Colosseum. It's incredible how overwhelming it is to walk next to so much history. The Colosseum's stones soak up a lot of heat, so this is where I felt most comfortable, weather-wise. Very pleasant. A few meters away, the Roman Forum and the Palatine begin. They're worth it too, absolutely; it's just that that's where the damn heat pierces the souls of eager walkers the most. The heat in Rome in July is terrible.

Then I went to see the third altar of science. Fire. The church of Santa Maria della Vittoria is where they burn the poor third _preferiti_ alive. It holds one of the sculptures I was most curious about, since Dan Brown makes so much of it: the _Ecstasy of Saint Teresa_, in which a horny angel pierces Saint Teresa with a "heavenly" arrow and gives her a brutal orgasm.

Finally, the fourth altar of science is Piazza Navona, where the fourth _preferiti_ is drowned in the fountain. Water. By the way, Ron Howard, who directed the film based on the book, adds an unexpected twist by letting the protagonist, Robert Langdon, rescue this _preferiti_, who ends up becoming pope. That doesn't happen in the book.

There's a lot to do in Rome, no doubt. But I focused on walking the Path and, along the way, I saw more than the troubled Robert Langdon did. Then again, there was no son-of-a-bitch assassin murdering people savagely and always escaping by a hair.

The end of the route is the Church of Illumination, Castel Sant'Angelo. It's worth noting that the dove on top of the obelisk in the Piazza Navona fountain points its beak toward the castle.
