I got the chance to leave Spain again. This time the destination was the neighbor of the last country I visited (the Netherlands): Belgium.

The weather was much kinder than in the Netherlands. This year we had an unusually long winter (or so the Europeans say) and, as a result, spring has been long too. I got to enjoy walks outdoors in fabulous parks, including a visit to one of the famous tulip gardens that usually open in spring around here (the Netherlands and part of Belgium). I tried the best beers in the world (and also the worst) and ate an amount of chocolate that was unusual even for me.

I have to say I loved Belgium, even though I only saw a small part of it. Specifically, I was in Brussels and Bruges. Bruges is a city out of a fairy tale, with its canals, its stone castles and its cobbled streets. It strikes me as the perfect place to be with a woman: the very air you breathe is charged with romance and desire. Which reminds me of the shops selling erotic chocolates and sweets around there. Wonderful! And I have nothing more to say on the subject.

As for the beer, it's mind-blowing. I went to the Delirium Café, the bar that holds the Guinness record for offering the largest variety of beers in the world. There I tried Tokio, the strongest beer in the world (18% ABV; and it isn't Japanese, it's British). It was disgusting. Of course, I also tried plenty of beers of supreme quality, and just as many fruity ones, like the coconut and chocolate ones.

The tulip gardens were spectacular, and that's coming from me, not exactly the kind of man who's into flowers and hearts. Really: anyone in the Netherlands or Belgium in spring shouldn't miss stopping by one of those gardens.

Finally, I want to mention my visit to one of the European monuments I had longed to see since I was a kid: the majestic Manneken Pis. It didn't disappoint, not by a single drop of pee! Simply fantastic. The Jeanneke Pis is another matter… Rather disturbing, actually. I don't recommend it.

Oh! I almost forgot to mention that I visited the Tintin museum, the detective comic character born from the imagination of the Belgian author Georges Remi (Hergé). I expected more from that museum. Maybe it didn't impress me because I never liked Tintin much. Boring Belgian, that one…
