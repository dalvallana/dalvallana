Now I'm going to tell you about one of the most spectacular cities I have ever visited: Berlin. A modern city. An ancient city. A cosmopolitan city. A historic city… Very historic. And what a story Berlin has to tell. A city forced to rise from its ashes, literally. A city that was sadly divided after being devastated. There was a wall in Berlin. An infamous wall. Berlin tells the story of a people forced to grow up in the cruelest way humanity has suffered in the history of war and peace.

As many of you know, after World War II, Germany fell under the control of the great victorious powers. The United States and the Soviet Union had allied to fight the Nazis, but they remained enemies after the war, since their political ideals were completely opposed. All the signs, notices and other texts around Berlin were now written in English, Russian and French. German became the least important language.

On top of that, with the Cold War, Soviet resources start to run out, and the consequences show in the decline of East Berlin. A brain drain begins, from the Soviet East to the American West, which further damages the already fragile Eastern economy. That's why they decide to build a sinister wall in Berlin. Friends and families are separated overnight. Many try to escape west by climbing the wall, but very few make it, because security is extreme and failing to cross means death. Nino Bravo's song "Libre" ("Free") is dedicated to one of the first victims the fucking wall claimed: Peter Fechter, a construction worker of just 18 who tried to cross the wall of shame and died trying.

<YouTube id="SFTGFu91B6E" title="Nino Bravo, “Libre”" />

Humiliated, separated, murdered… A people reduced to nothing kept paying for the disasters caused by a deranged ruler for more than 40 years after his death.

The fall of the Berlin Wall in '89 is one of the most important and influential events in human history. I simply find it incredible that a people reduced to dust can find the strength to get up, fight and work until it becomes the engine of Europe once again. This Paul van Dyk music video captures perfectly why I admire the German people.

Berlin never forgets. Even in the most remote corners of the city you will always find something that makes you aware of the sad history hidden behind this wonderful city. Even while drinking a divine Paulaner on the Kurfürstendamm, you have the dubious honor of seeing the Memorial Church, whose main tower was destroyed by a bomb and never rebuilt.

On many sidewalks in the city you can also see small golden plaques set into the ground, with names engraved on them. They are the names of the Jews who lived in the building right in front of you and who were taken by force to concentration camps.

I want to stress how incredible this city is. I urge you, dear reader, to take the first chance you get to pay a planned (or unplanned) visit to this wonderful metropolis, which will no doubt leave you in love (or obsessed), as it has me.

Today Berlin is one of the most important cities in Europe economically, and also one of the most fun, because it doesn't matter if you're a geek, a skater, a hipster, a Latino, an Asian, a Hindu, a Muslim, a gay man or a gay Jewish salsa-dancing Latino: you will always find a corner of Berlin that makes you feel you're in the right place.
